{% blocktrans %}This policy explains what personal data the Universal Bicycle Team website collects, why we collect it, and how you can have it removed. The site is a free, non-commercial project for the amateur cycling and running community.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "What we collect" %}

{% blocktrans %}Account data. When you register we store your email address and a cryptographic hash of your password (never the password itself). You may optionally add your name, date of birth, gender, city, team, a link to your Strava profile, and your preferred sports and locations, so that forms are pre-filled for you, and your sports achievements, such as a rank or category.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Sign-in through Google, GitHub or Strava. If you use one of these buttons, we receive only your identifier with that service and your name, which we use to create or sign in to your account. From Strava we receive your athlete identifier and name only: we do not request access to your activities, routes, segments or any training data, and we never see them.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Your agreement. Whoever creates an account agrees with this policy: on the sign-up form, or, for an account made from a sign-in with Google, GitHub or Strava, on a page shown once at the first sign-in. We keep when you agreed.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Virtual rides. When you are signed in, the rides you finish on the virtual ride page are saved to your profile: the route, time, distance, speed, power, cadence and heart rate, and the track of your position along the route. While you are on that page, other signed-in members see your name, what you are riding and where you are on the route. If you connect Strava for your rides, we keep the access Strava gives us for this and use it only to upload each ride you finish as a virtual ride; we still do not read your Strava activities. You can disconnect Strava in your profile at any time, and we then revoke that access.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Race registration data. When you register for an event we store the details the organizer requires: name, date of birth, gender, city, team, category, and an optional free-text field (some organizers use it for a link to your Strava profile).{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Content you publish. Events, comments, reports about inaccurate events, and files uploaded by organizers such as regulations, route files and result protocols.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Technical data. A session cookie that keeps you signed in, cookies remembering your chosen language and colour theme and your browser's time zone, and ordinary server logs. We do not use advertising or analytics trackers, and there is no third-party tracking code on the site.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Visit counts. The site counts its readers itself, with no third party. When a page opens, your browser tells the site which page it is and which site you came from, and the site adds one to that day's totals: of pages, of hours of the day, of the sites people come from, of phones and computers, and of languages. Nothing about you is kept -- no cookie, no IP address, no identifier -- only the sums, which lead back to nobody. Programs such as search engines are counted by the name they give. The pages the site's administrators and owners open are counted apart, for each of them, so that the owners see how the site is run.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Why we use it" %}

{% blocktrans %}To run your account, to let you register for events and to pass the registration details to that event's organizer, to publish the race calendar, start lists and results, to moderate submissions, and to send you service messages such as email confirmation and password reset.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "What is visible to others" %}

{% blocktrans %}Sport results are public by nature. Start lists and result protocols show participants' names and the details the organizer chose to publish, such as city, team and category. The organizer of an event can see the registration data of the people registered for it. Your email address is never shown publicly. Comments you leave are public and are shown with your name.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}People who manage your registrations and photos. You may let another member manage your race registrations and race photos: they send you a link, you allow it on the page it opens, and they accept your request. That member then sees your last name, first name, date of birth, gender, city and team, and the Strava link an event's form may ask for, but never your email address; they can register you for events, change and cancel your registrations, and see and mark your race photos. Either of you can end this in the profile at any time: from then on that member sees nothing of yours and can do nothing for you, and the registrations they made stay yours.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}People without an account. A member may add people who have no account on the site -- the children of their section, their family -- with their last name, first name, gender, date of birth, team, city, Strava link and sports achievements, to register them for events and to look after their race photos. Before adding anyone the member confirms that they may handle these people's personal data: they have the consent of the people themselves or of their legal representatives, and they answer for having it. On the site only that member sees and changes these people's data; the organizer of an event sees what a registration for it holds, as for anyone registered, and start lists and results show them as they show everyone. When the member deletes such a person, their registrations for races to come are cancelled and past races keep them in the results; when the member's account is deleted, the people they added are deleted with it.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Handing a person over. A person added this way may take their data into an account of their own: the member sends them a link, they open it signed in, and the member confirms it. Their registrations and the bibs named for their photos then go to that account, its empty profile fields are filled from what the member typed in, and the member no longer sees or changes them -- unless the person lets the member go on managing their registrations and photos.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Reviews of events. After an event you took part in, the site asks you to rate it. Your account keeps only that you answered -- with a review or with a refusal, which cannot be told apart -- and not when. The review keeps nothing of who wrote it or when: nobody, the site's owners included, can see that it is yours, and so it cannot be changed or deleted afterwards. Its score goes into the organizer's public rating; the site shows no review as it was written.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}The digest of reviews. For the people who edit an event -- its author, the site's admins and owners --, the scores and the words of its reviews, shuffled and with nothing that names their authors, go to the language model provider the site's assistant uses, which sums them up. The site keeps that digest and shows it to them alone, from two reviews up.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "What we never do" %}

{% blocktrans %}We do not sell, rent or trade your data. We do not use it for advertising and we do not use it to train machine-learning or AI models. We do not share it with anyone except the service providers listed below, the organizer of an event you are registered for, and the members you allowed to manage your registrations and photos.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Service providers" %}

{% blocktrans %}The site runs on a commercial hosting platform and uses an email provider to deliver service messages; both process data solely on our behalf. Map tiles are loaded from OpenStreetMap, and some interface libraries are loaded from public content delivery networks, which means those services see your IP address as part of serving the request.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}Your region. If you are not signed in, the calendar, the list and the map open on the events of your region, when it can be told. The network provider that delivers the site works out an approximate location from your IP address and passes it to us with the request; we use it only to choose your region as the page opens and do not store it. A cookie that lasts until you close the browser remembers that the region was offered, so that once you clear it you keep the whole calendar.{% endblocktrans %}

{% blocktrans %}The assistant. The site's organizers and administrators may write events by talking to an assistant on their pages, and administrators news as well. What they write to it, and the form as it stands, go to a language-model provider, which prepares the texts; the conversation is kept on the site with its event or news item.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Storage and deletion" %}

{% blocktrans %}We keep your data while your account exists. You can edit or clear most of it yourself in your profile at any time, and you can disconnect a linked Google, GitHub or Strava account from that service's settings. If you ask us to delete your account, we remove your personal data, including any identifier and name received from Strava. Published results of past events may be kept for historical accuracy in the same way they appear in ordinary sport protocols.{% endblocktrans %}

{% trans "Your rights" %}

{% blocktrans %}You can ask us what data we hold about you, ask for it to be corrected, or ask for it to be deleted. Write to us and we will respond.{% endblocktrans %}
